Card Mechanics & Rules

Card text is the final word on what an individual card does. The sections below explain the common patterns you will see while playing.

Timing windows

Cards and abilities are not available at every moment. Your Main phase is the normal window for playing cards and using most activated abilities. Combat has its own attacker and blocker decisions. Some effects open an interrupt window after a card is played; an opponent can respond only if they have a legal action and can pay its cost. A card may also specify a narrower timing window.

Three common ability patterns

  • Static ability: continuously changes a rule while the card is in play, such as how a Unit attacks or what can target it.
  • Triggered ability: happens when its stated event occurs. A Battlecry triggers when a Unit enters from your hand; Spawn can trigger when a supported permanent enters from another zone; Deathrattle triggers when a Unit is defeated.
  • Activated ability: an action you choose to use when its timing and cost requirements are satisfied. The ability may tap its source or have a once-per-turn limit; read the card.

Attacking and blocking

In structured combat, the active player declares attackers and confirms the attack. Opponents who are targeted then assign legal blockers. Combat damage and related defeat effects wait until required blocker choices have been recorded. A Unit normally cannot attack on the turn it enters play without Charge or Haste, but it can still block.

Lane, depth, range, movement, and keywords can restrict which attackers, blockers, and targets are legal. A card that is ready is not automatically allowed to do everything; the exact board position and current phase matter.

Effects and reactions

Cards can cause effects directly or queue triggered effects. Those effects resolve in a defined order, and an effect may require you to choose a legal target. Elemental exposure and reactions can create additional interactions; their results depend on the triggering Elements and the relevant card or Hero text.

How to read a rule

  1. Check when the ability can happen.
  2. Check its cost and any once-per-turn or rank restriction.
  3. Check the target and whether it must be chosen or is automatic.
  4. Follow any duration, “if able,” or “instead” language exactly.

The engine rejects illegal actions, so a highlighted target or enabled action is the reliable guide to what is currently legal. For keywords on your own cards, use the full card inspection text rather than relying on the short label alone.
