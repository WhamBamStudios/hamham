# First Match: Rules & Mechanics

## The goal

A standard match seats two to four players. Protect your Hero and outlast the opposition. A player is eliminated when their Hero reaches 0 Life or they concede; the last Hero standing wins. If every remaining Hero is eliminated at the same time, the match is a draw.

## Starting a match

You begin with a hand of six cards. When the match enables a mulligan, you may return any number of those cards and draw replacements before turn one. The first player starts the first turn without drawing an extra card; after that, a player normally draws one card at the start of each turn. If a deck runs out, drawing causes fatigue damage. The usual maximum hand size is six, with excess cards discarded at end of turn.

## A turn at a glance

1. **Start of Turn:** resolve start-of-turn effects, ready eligible pieces, and reset once-per-turn actions.
2. **Environment:** play one Domain from your hand or pass. Some effects can put a Domain into play outside this step.
3. **Aeon Generation:** your Hero and other cards produce elemental Aeon for the turn.
4. **Draw:** draw a card (except on turn one, when the opening hand already serves as your draw).
5. **Main:** play cards, use eligible abilities, and activate your Hero Power.
6. **Combat / Attack:** declare attackers. Defenders assign legal blockers; combat damage resolves after required block choices are complete.
7. **End of Turn:** resolve end-of-turn effects, discard down to the hand limit if needed, and pass play.

The game may skip a decision step when there is no legal choice to make. Timers or mode-specific settings can also affect how long a decision is available.

## The battlefield

In the standard lane-enabled format, each side has five lanes with three positions in each lane. Position matters: card abilities, range, movement, and lane effects can change which attacks and targets are legal. A newly played Unit can usually block right away, but cannot attack or use an active ability on the turn it arrives unless it has **Charge** or **Haste**.

## Paying for cards

Cards have an elemental Aeon cost. Some symbols require one named Element, some allow any Element, and paired symbols let you choose between two named Elements. See [Elemental Aeon](elemental-aeon.md) for the payment basics.

Playing a card is not always the end of the conversation: if an opponent has a legal, affordable response, an interrupt window may open. Combat, triggers, and card-specific text can create additional decisions.
